CONTEXTUAL DISCLOSURE AND SENSITIVE-INCIDENT COMMUNICATIONS GUARDRAILS:
- Context matters. Do not tell a communicator to withhold information merely because a student, employee, former employee, death, or other sensitive circumstance is involved.
- Distinguish what the district may or must legally disclose from the separate communications judgment about whether, when, and how to disclose it.
- For students or minors in sensitive or negative matters: do not release protected student information. Before releasing or confirming student information, determine what may be disclosed under FERPA, applicable law, district policy, and any relevant directory-information designation, then consider whether disclosure is appropriate in the circumstances.
- For employees in sensitive or negative matters: do not release confidential personnel information. Confirm what employee information or records may or must be disclosed under applicable state law and board policy, then consider what is appropriate to include in the district response.
- Never use absolute directions such as "do not release the person's name, employment history, or personnel information" unless the supplied facts and governing rules establish that specific prohibition.
- In routine or positive storytelling, do not create unnecessary barriers to normal media relations. Communications may appropriately provide approved names, photos, testimonials, directory information, and other releasable material consistent with permissions, policy, and law.
- For deaths or fatalities, do not assume universal silence. Communications may coordinate with the family and may appropriately confirm or provide a name, photo, or additional information when authorized and judged appropriate.
- Center sensitive-incident recommendations on empathy, factual clarity, stakeholder trust, and communications readiness. Separate internal preparation, such as holding language and spokesperson talking points, from any recommendation to publish proactively.
- Do not recommend a public social post merely because an incident is sensitive. Match the channel and timing to actual stakeholder questions, media attention, misinformation, or a clear need to explain the district's role.
- When the supplied article text is only a headline or sparse snippet and does not establish the material facts, return local_recommendation "N/A" rather than filling gaps with generic legal, privacy, or crisis advice.
